# Pitch Voice and Jargon

## Default voice

Use a **premium, consultative, authoritative, and earned** voice when PRIME is the brand or the user has not chosen another voice. “Premium” means clear judgment, precise details, restraint, and respect for the audience's time. It does not mean ornate language, inflated claims, or self-importance.

- Lead with the audience's issue, decision, or consequence.
- State the recommendation plainly and support it with a visible reason.
- Sound confident without promising outcomes the evidence cannot support.
- Name the trade-off and explain when the offer is or is not worthwhile.
- Respect the audience's competence. Do not patronize, scold, flatter, or manufacture urgency.
- Keep sentences easy to say aloud. Prefer strong verbs, concrete nouns, and short transitions.
- Keep PRIME's “We Advise. You Advance.” line available as a brand signature, not a mandatory tagline on every deck.

For another brand or audience, follow their supplied voice guide or sample. Ask one focused question if the voice could change the pitch's credibility.

## Jargon policy

Jargon is useful when it is the audience's working language or when it preserves a material distinction. It is wasteful when it signals expertise without helping a decision.

1. Identify terms the audience uses in real work. Prefer those over generic corporate language.
2. Expand acronyms on first use, unless every intended reader plainly knows them or the deck has a separate glossary.
3. Translate specialist terms on first use when a board member, client, or cross-functional stakeholder might not know them. Example: “TPV (total project value, the full value of the contracted project).” Use the short form consistently after that.
4. Preserve exact meaning. Do not interchange revenue, gross transaction value, collected fees, net commission, target credit, margin, pipeline, and forecast.
5. Keep the main slide language accessible. Put definitions in a note or glossary when they would interrupt the argument.
6. Use a glossary only when several terms recur; do not add one to make ordinary words look technical.
7. Never stuff acronyms into headings or use unexplained abbreviations to save a few words.

For a PRIME real-estate or referral pitch, use terms such as **referral fee, commission share, collected fee, accredited contractor, client brief, target credit, transaction volume,** and **contractor scope** only when they are accurate for the source. Define terms such as **TPV** at first use. Keep statements about crediting, fees, accreditation, or process ownership tied to the supplied policy or source document; do not treat a deck example as company policy.

## Words to question

Replace or prove phrases that could appear in any company's deck: “game changer,” “unlock value,” “seamless,” “world-class,” “best-in-class,” “innovative solution,” “end-to-end,” “synergy,” “robust,” “revolutionary,” “transformative,” “future-proof,” “one-stop shop,” “at scale,” and “value-add.” These are not automatic bans; keep one only when it is a precise, sourced phrase that this audience uses and needs.

Avoid empty intensifiers (“very,” “highly,” “significantly”), unsupported superlatives, vague promises, canned transitions, fake quotations, and headline fragments that hide who benefits. Replace them with the actual action, threshold, evidence, or consequence.

## Slide language check

For each slide, ask:

- Could the title identify this deck or slide specifically, or is it generic?
- Does the first sentence explain why this matters to this audience?
- Does every technical term earn its space and have a clear meaning?
- Can the speaker say the copy naturally in one breath or two?
- Is any phrase making a stronger claim than the evidence supports?
- Can a skeptical listener distinguish fact, assumption, and illustration?
