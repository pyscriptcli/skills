# PRIME Pitch Codex

Create a deck that helps a specific audience make a specific decision. Build the argument before choosing the slides or styling. The deck may be for any industry or use case; PRIME branding is the default only when the user asks for PRIME or the source material is clearly PRIME-branded.

## Working modes

- **Grill and build (default when the brief has meaningful gaps):** use the one-question-at-a-time interview in [references/grill-me.md](references/grill-me.md). Be thorough enough to expose a weak case, but never ask for information already given or collect details that cannot change the pitch.
- **Build now:** when the user explicitly asks to skip discovery, or has already supplied a complete brief, proceed. State only material assumptions and keep the work moving.
- **Review or refine:** preserve the supplied deck's purpose and useful structure; diagnose the argument, audience fit, language, evidence, design, and requested file format before changing it.

The interview is not a permission gate. Once the user has supplied enough to create a useful draft, summarize the brief and build it. Keep unresolved points visible as assumptions, placeholders, or questions for the audience; do not invent answers.

## Build the pitch

1. Establish the audience, decision, realistic alternative, strongest objection, and proposed next action. Define the benefit from the audience's point of view, including what they give up, risk, or need to do.
2. Trace the value logic: audience need → specific mechanism → practical outcome → evidence. If that chain has a weak or unsupported link, surface it. Never disguise an internal aspiration as a proven result.
3. Use the supplied material first. Distinguish documented facts, user-stated premises, interpretation, proposal, and hypothetical example. Verify time-sensitive or externally checkable claims using reliable sources; cite them beside the claim or in slide notes. Do not invent metrics, customers, testimonials, credentials, approvals, guarantees, or case studies.
4. Show economics when they affect the decision. Compare equivalent cases, preserve units and time periods, identify gross/net and contracted/collected amounts, show assumptions, and calculate a break-even point when inputs support it. Label illustrative arithmetic; never turn it into a forecast.
5. Give every slide one job. Make titles state a useful subject or an evidence-supported takeaway. Choose a story order that fits this decision; do not force a fixed slide count or standard pitch formula. Include risks, limits, ownership, or implementation only when they affect the decision.
6. Select diagrams, tables, comparisons, timelines, and charts because they clarify the argument. Use the requested brand and format. Keep editable content editable where the format supports it.
7. Close with the action the audience should take, who owns it, and what happens next. Avoid an empty recap in place of an ask.

## Voice and terminology

Default to a premium, consultative PRIME voice: assured, measured, specific, commercially literate, and earned by evidence. Be candid about trade-offs. Use persuasion through relevance, logic, proof, and a clear next step; do not pressure or posture. Keep the voice human and direct, not ceremonious. Adapt to the audience and to any voice sample the user provides. Read [references/voice-and-jargon.md](references/voice-and-jargon.md) before drafting substantial pitch copy.

Use industry language when it makes a material distinction clearer. Expand an acronym at first use, then use it consistently. Pair specialized terms with a brief plain-language explanation when some of the audience may not know them. Keep exact contractual, financial, technical, or regulatory meaning intact; plain language must not blur a material distinction. Ask which expressions the user wants preferred or avoided when that choice changes the pitch.

## PRIME identity

When the deck is PRIME-branded, apply [references/prime-brand.md](references/prime-brand.md) and use the official PRIME brand skill or current brand source if available. Use official logos with contrast suited to the background. Do not invent or redraw brand assets. Do not imply PRIME has approved a proposal unless the supplied source confirms approval.

## Anti-slop standard

Read [references/anti-slop.md](references/anti-slop.md) while shaping the story and design. Every claim, phrase, and visual must do a job for this audience. Prefer a sharp, supported argument over polished-sounding filler. Avoid generic slogans, canned transitions, repeated summaries, feature dumps, repeated card grids, decorative stock imagery, ornamental icons, and charts without evidence. Vary the slide composition only when variation helps the story. Do not copy a recognizable deck or brand's signature layout.

## Create and verify

Use the user's requested output format and dimensions. For HTML decks, make slide navigation keyboard-accessible, provide visible focus, support print/save-to-PDF, and check narrow screens. For PowerPoint or Google Slides, follow the presentation skill/tooling available and keep text, tables, and shapes editable where practical. For PDF conversion, preserve the source aspect ratio, slide order, logo contrast, typography, and layout; if an exact renderer or font is unavailable, say so and provide the closest verified result without calling it pixel-identical.

Inspect every rendered slide after the final edit. Check the actual fonts and weights, type hierarchy, text fit, clipping, overlap, line breaks, contrast, image quality, logo treatment, page count, and source notes. Recalculate visible arithmetic independently. Run relevant build, export, or validation checks. Do not claim a visual or interaction check that was not performed. Report material limitations plainly.
